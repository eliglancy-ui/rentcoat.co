The story

What is the title of liberty?

In Alma 46, a man named Amalickiah is trying to make himself king, and the Nephites are close to losing their freedom. Captain Moroni, the chief commander of the Nephite armies, tears his own coat, writes on a piece of it, and fastens it to the end of a pole.

“In memory of our God, our religion, and freedom, and our peace, our wives, and our children.”Alma 46:12

He calls the banner the title of liberty (Alma 46:13), prays for liberty to rest on his people, and goes out among them waving it so everyone can see what he wrote (Alma 46:19). The people come running and tear their own garments as a promise that they won't forsake the Lord (Alma 46:21). Later Moroni has the title of liberty raised on every tower in the land (Alma 46:36).

The same chapter is where the Book of Mormon says the true believers in Christ “took upon them, gladly, the name of Christ, or Christians as they were called” (Alma 46:15).

“Rent” is the old word for torn. Rent Coat Co. is named for that moment, and the flag in our logo is that banner: take what you have and turn it into something you stand behind.

What did Captain Moroni write on the title of liberty?

Alma 46:12 says he wrote: “In memory of our God, our religion, and freedom, and our peace, our wives, and our children.” Then he fastened it to the end of a pole and, in Alma 46:13, called it the title of liberty.

Where is the title of liberty in the Book of Mormon?

Alma 46:11–36. Moroni makes the banner in verses 12 and 13, waves it among the people in verse 19, and has it raised on every tower in the land in verse 36.
